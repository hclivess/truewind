        First sail

        • The wind blows from the top of the map (the white arrow). No boat can sail straight into it: keep at least about 45° off the wind, or the sails flap and you stop (“in irons”).
        • Steer with AD or ←→ (on a phone: the ◀ ▶ buttons). The helm stays where you leave it — Space centres it.
        • Press T for automatic trim and the crew set every sail for you. Take the sheets yourself later: WS mainsheet, ↑↓ jib.
        • Telltales: LUFF trim in or bear away · FLOW good · STALL ease out or head up. Heeling hard? Ease the main, or reef (R).

        CleatsEvery line is held by what the real boat has: cam or clam cleat, V-jammer, horn cleat, clutch, ratchet block, car lock or winch. Its button in the panel (or the part on deck) makes it fast or casts it off, each in its own time — a cam at once, a horn cleat's figure-eights in seconds. Released, a loaded line dumps (cam, clutch) or surges round the drum (winch, horn); a ratchet leaves it in your hand. Hauling goes through a cam, clutch or ratchet; easing, or hauling off a horn cleat, takes the line off first. Overloaded, a cleat slips.

        Reading the boat

        Telltales in the rig panel show each third of the sail: LUFF ease less or bear away, FLOW right on, STALL ease the sheet or head up. Upwind, twist is set by mainsheet tension and the jib lead; the angle by the traveler. When overpowered: backstay on, cunningham on, outhaul on, traveler down, then reef.
